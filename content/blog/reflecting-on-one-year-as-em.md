It's been one year since I stepped into the role of Engineering Manager. Well, Feb 15 2021 was Presidents' Day and a day off at work, but that was the Monday of my first week ramping up as EM.

The previous manager on my team recently learned he would have to take medical leave for around 6 months, and the team needed someone to step in and help the team out. I was already working on spinning up a new team that I would lead in a few months, so it wasn't a completely unprompted change of roles, just an accelerated timeline.

By the way, my previous manager recovered completely fine and has returned to work. He's doing well. :)

Here's how I felt each quarter went and the most important lesson from each.

## 2021 Q1

My first quarter as an EM was chaotic. I rapidly shifted focus from starting a new team to leading the team I was already on. I quickly learned we were behind on planning a project - at least that's how it felt - and we needed to scope it so we could complete quarterly planning for Q2.

Balancing speed of scoping and accuracy was most challenging. My Product Manager was pushing me to get estimates sooner than later and devs were pushing for more time to clear up unknowns. We already knew it was going to be a monumental project. I learned later on (in part thanks to quarterly conversations) that I did not do a great job managing the team here. I was pushing devs to accept too much uncertainty and wasn't holding my ground defending my team enough. While it's good to push folks to expand their comfort zone a little and be able to work around uncertainties, what manifested was a project that took about 2 months more than estimated.

Without getting too deep into the weeds of the project, there were a lot of parts of the project that were very greenfield and used new technologies to our team. We didn't account for just how complicated some of these pieces would be and our timelines kept growing as things got figured out. In addition, project management took significantly longer due to how many moving parts there were. We were rebuilding a whole system on multiple platforms across several different teams. Coordination takes time.

*Learning*: Trust your team and defend them. They're closer to the code than you.

*Learning 2*: Break big things into really small ones. Encouraging smaller deliverables at 6 week intrervals could have gotten folks into a mindset where we build more iteratively rather than having to figure everything out from the beginning.
