In the last several months, I've had multiple people ask about why I became an Engineering Manager in order to see if they wanted to follow the same path.

First, this is absolutely the right thing to do when considering any kind of change. Talk to the folks doing the job you want to really get a better feel for if you'd actually enjoy it. In fact, talking to other EMs is what made me go from considering to decided. Also, talk to as many folks as you can. Get lots of different perspectives, because everyone's journey is different and has varying degrees of complexity, stress, politics, easiness, product involvement, etc. 

Disclaimer, below are several things to consider and help guauge if you have intentions that will help you be successful in the role or ones that could make the role harder for you. At the end of the day, it's up to you if you think you'd be happier in the role.

---

### 1. You want to support others more directly

This is only first because it was my reason for becoming an EM. As an EM, you have the direct ability to help coach, encourage, and develop people on your team - not to mention give them *more money!*

If this is a reason you want to become an EM, you're likely an empathetic person. **This can be both a strength and a weakness.** Caring and putting your all in for your reports will be easy until you have to deal with performance management. There will be times where you'll have to practice [radical candor](https://www.radicalcandor.com/radical-candor-not-brutal-honesty/) to get yourself in the right mindset that having difficult conversations is better than not addressing issues.
